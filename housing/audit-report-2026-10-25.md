## Headline findings — corrections strong enough to send
These are the items with own-site evidence, material to someone trying
to use the service. All other items are in the per-listing table below.

1. **HELP Ministries RAP — funding is NOT guaranteed; call first.**
   Own site (ccuhbg.org/what-we-do/help-ministries.html, fetched
   2026-10-25): RAP is for Dauphin County residents seeking back rent
   or move-in help (security deposit), by appointment (call
   717-238-2851; walk-ins Mon/Wed/Thu 10:30am and 2:30pm; arrive 15
   minutes early), at 413 S. 19th St, Harrisburg, PA 17104. Critically:
   "There are times where RAP funding is not available. We encourage
   any applicants to call the HELP Ministries Office to ensure funding
   availability." Any directory line implying RAP funds are currently
   available must carry that date-stamped caveat. RAP eligibility
   (own-site qualifications document) requires Dauphin County
   residency, homelessness or a court-ordered Order of Possession,
   landlord agreement, proof of income, and prior receipt of all
   entitled ESA funds — back-rent applicants must not be in subsidised
   housing.
2. **Dauphin County Housing Authority HCV waitlist is CLOSED on
   2026-10-25 — own-site statement.** Own site
   (dauphinhousing.org/hcv-rental-assistance, fetched 2026-10-25):
   "Our HCV (Section 8) waitlist is currently closed. You will not be
   able to apply at this time." Tenant pays 30%–40% of adjusted gross
   monthly income at initial lease-up; voucher usable anywhere in
   Dauphin County OUTSIDE the City of Harrisburg, with 90 days to
   locate housing. Authority: 501 Mohn St, Steelton, PA 17113,
   717-939-9301. Do not list this voucher as open.
3. **Bridge of Hope Harrisburg Area — NOT emergency rental assistance;
   use the referral line, not the directory phone.** Own site
   (harrisburg.bridgeofhopeinc.org/get-help/, fetched 2026-10-25):
   "PLEASE KNOW THAT WE ARE NOT AN EMERGENCY SHELTER, AND WE DO NOT
   PROVIDE EMERGENCY RENTAL ASSISTANCE." It is a 12–24 month programme
   for single mothers/children facing homelessness in Cumberland,
   Dauphin, and York counties: housing within 60 days, decreasing
   rental assistance, case management, 6–10 Neighboring Volunteers.
   Referral line: (717) 303-9930 (messages checked twice a week).
   The directory phone 717-635-5957 appears only in the page footer
   as a general number — the referral line is the intake route.
   Intake takes several weeks; no guarantee of acceptance.
4. **Dauphin County ERAP is NOT live help — remove it from current
   listings.** County's own news: ERAP paused new applications
   effective end of business March 4, 2022 ("we will reach the end of
   state and federal funding"); a later reopening ran only July 15–
   30, 2024 (dauphincounty.gov news, July 12, 2024). No open ERAP
   programme was found on 2026-10-25. Any directory still listing
   Dauphin ERAP as an application route is stale.
5. **Catholic Charities Harrisburg — do NOT list as a rent-grant
   provider on this evidence.** Secondary summary of the diocesan
   agency states it provides referrals to housing/shelter (and
   counselling, immigration help) but "do[es] not provide financial
   aid." Own contact confirmed: 4800 Union Deposit Rd, Harrisburg,
   PA 17110, 717-657-4804, cchbg.org (15-county Diocese of Harrisburg).
   List it, if at all, as referrals/counselling via that phone — not
   as rent money. (Verdict CLARIFIED, not CORRECTED, because the
   "no financial aid" wording rests on a secondary summary, not a
   completed own-site fetch.)
6. **Salvation Army Harrisburg — list by phone and appointment, not
   by a single address.** Phone 717-233-6755 is consistent across
   sources, with appointment ext. 135 in a secondary listing.
   Addresses conflict: the county housing authority's services page
   gives 1122 Green St, Harrisburg, PA 17102, while the Army's own
   regional listing and utility-assistance pages give 506 S. 29th St,
   Harrisburg, PA 17104 (utility office hours Mon–Fri 8:00am–4:30pm).
   Treat 506 S. 29th St as the service centre and 1122 Green St as
   unresolved/secondary until an own-site page confirms it; rent help
   is described everywhere as limited.
7. **YWCA housing intake runs through HELP, not through the YWCA
   front desk.** Own site (ywcahbg.org/programs/housing-and-
   homelessness/, fetched 2026-10-25): to apply for a YWCA residential
   programme, call the HELP office at 717-238-2851 and request a
   housing assessment; YWCA direct housing contact is Christina Free,
   717-234-7931 ext. 3052. Programmes: Bridge/Transitional Housing,
   Permanent Housing–Disabilities (rental assistance + weekly case
   management until Section 8/public housing/self-sufficiency),
   Safe Haven (chronically homeless women, mental-health diagnosis),
   Section 8 Single Room Occupancy. Counties served: Dauphin,
   Cumberland, Perry. Address 1101 Market St, Harrisburg, PA 17103.
   This is the duplicate-route flag: HELP (item 1) is the front door
   for YWCA housing (item 8), not a separate rent fund.
8. **Harrisburg vs Dauphin County housing authorities are separate
   jurisdictions — do not merge them.** Harrisburg Housing Authority
   serves the City of Harrisburg only (351 Chestnut St, Harrisburg,
   PA 17101, 717-232-6781; Section 8 office phone 717-234-9664 on the
   county housing page). Dauphin County Housing Authority serves
   Dauphin County outside the city (501 Mohn St, Steelton, PA 17113,
   717-939-9301). Secondary waitlist aggregators report both Section 8
   lists closed on 2026-10-25 and Harrisburg senior/disabled public
   housing open — that split is SECONDARY-ONLY and must not be sent
   as fact without an own-site check of harrisburghousing.org, which
   was not completed in this audit.

## Duplicate / shared-route flags (do not double-count)
- One county voucher authority, two programmes: items 3 (HCV) and 4
  (public housing / Shelter+Care) are the same Housing Authority of
  the County of Dauphin — count as one agency, two programmes.
- One front door, two listings: HELP Ministries (item 1) is the
  housing-assessment intake for YWCA housing (item 8) and for
  congregate emergency shelters — not a second rent fund.
- One coalition, many providers: CACH (item 14) coordinates the
  Continuum of Care; its permanent-housing page lists other items'
  programmes (YWCA, Shalom House, Dauphin County HA Shelter+Care,
  Safe Haven) — do not count CACH itself as an additional provider.
- City vs county authorities: Harrisburg Housing Authority (item 2)
  and Dauphin County Housing Authority (items 3–4) are separate
  agencies with separate jurisdictions and waitlists.
